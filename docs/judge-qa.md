# Hard questions judges will ask

| Question | Our answer |
|---|---|
| How big is this market? | Small businesses in emerging markets face a **$5.7T finance gap** (IFC). The repay-from-sales model is proven: **Shopify Capital $4.2B** a year, **Mercado Pago $2.9B** in merchant loans, Moniepoint about $700M, M-Pesa's Fuliza. They do it by owning your data and your payment rails; we do it with no data, open rails, and a credit history the merchant owns. Our real bottleneck is stablecoin acceptance at the till, and the advance is the reason to switch. |
| Why World ID if the borrower is a business? | We don't use it to verify the business: the demo uses a mock attestor and fictional seller portal; real Reclaim is built and tested, and one-per-human would wrongly block owners of several businesses (one human may bind several shops). World sits at the human moments: **Agent Pass**, when a merchant delegates once to their managed cash-flow agent (after that, credit is fully automatic), and **IDKit**, so only the same human can recover their private credentials on a new phone, and **IDKit before the first advance**, so a defaulter can't come back under a new pseudonym. |
| What stops a merchant switching to another payment address? | Nothing fully stops it, and this is our main weakness. We limit the damage with small first advances, limits that grow only through repayment, and alerts on sudden sales drops. The Standing Pass makes good behaviour valuable, because a repaid credential is worth more than one skipped repayment. |
| Who are the merchants today? | Merchants in dollar-short economies, such as Argentina, Nigeria and Kenya, where people already hold stablecoins to escape inflation. The advance gives them a reason to accept stablecoins at the till. |
| Customers there use USDT, not USDC. | Live checkout takes test USDC only. Any-token checkout swaps are planned. |
| What does ZK actually hide? | Who you are (World ID), your borrowing history (Standing Pass), and in the stretch version your offchain books (zkTLS). Repayment on a known shop is still public. Hiding that needs a shielded pool, which is our roadmap. |
| Why not just use a KYC'd lender? | Contact-harvesting loan apps show what happens when lenders hold borrower data. Our privacy is built into the system, not a policy we promise to follow. Selective disclosure covers regulators. |
| Can sales be faked to get a bigger advance? | Yes, through wash trading. The demo catches some direct circular funding, but intermediary wallets and self-funded sales can evade checks. The Critic and caps do not establish genuine customers. |
| Can a prompt injection drain the pool? | No. Agent output is schema-bound, the Critic must approve independently, and `AdvanceBook` caps every draw onchain. We show it failing in the demo. |
| What stops a defaulter from opening a new shop? | **World ID.** Before a shop's first advance, its owner proves "unique human" with World ID (IDKit, Proof of Human). Our offer signer keeps only an HMAC of the World nullifier, linked privately and encrypted to that human's shops. If any advance is written off, that human is banned: a brand-new pseudonymous shop with a fresh Business Pass still gets `human_banned`, before any AI is called and with no money moved (the simulator's `defaulter` run shows it). A human also can't hold two open advances across shops (`human_has_open_advance`). Nothing about the person goes onchain and the API never returns the nullifier. The honest limit: the ban list is enforced by our signer, not the contract; the roadmap is a ZK "not in the banned set" proof. |
| Can a defaulter come back with a new wallet? | Not as the same human: the World ID ban covers every shop they open (see above). Separately, a Standing Pass is issued only on full repayment and its nullifier prevents reuse of that credential. Someone borrowing another person's World ID remains possible, as with any proof-of-personhood system. |
| How do limits grow? | Gradually, per human. The first advance at a new limit level is capped at 50% of it (tier 1: $50 of $100); each full week of on-time minimum repayments (1% plus fee per 7 days, replayed from onchain repayment events) unlocks another 25%, so the full limit comes after two good weeks. A missed minimum freezes it; a write-off bans. |
| Is this predatory? | Merchant advances can be expensive, so the fee is small and shown upfront: 0.5% a week while you owe, never more than 3%, with slow, normal and busy repayment estimates. There are no rollovers. A bad week only slows repayment, as long as the merchant meets the 1% weekly minimum. Fall behind it and the fee doubles to 1% a week (max 6% total) until they catch up, and that extra goes 100% to the safety reserve, not to lenders. |
| Why would anyone trade the junior token? | It earns fees for taking first loss. Liquidity would be thin at first, so the demo seeds the pool. |
| Is it legal? | A real launch needs licensing in each market (for example, Kenya licenses digital lenders) and KYC through selective disclosure. AI credit decisions face extra scrutiny, including the EU AI Act's high-risk rules. |
| Isn't the anonymity set tiny? | In a demo, yes. Privacy grows with the number of repaid advances, which is why the Standing Pass should be portable across lenders. |
