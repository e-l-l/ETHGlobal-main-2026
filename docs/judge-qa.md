# Hard questions judges will ask

| Question | Our answer |
|---|---|
| What isn't private yet? | Sale and advance amounts, the shop's payout address, and every write are public under a pseudonymous shop ID. The World ID ban list is held privately by our offer signer, not proven in ZK, and the issuer briefly sees the seller-account ID during the zkTLS check. Our servers keep no IP logs. Each gap has a concrete design and size in [FUTURE.md](FUTURE.md): stealth payouts (ERC-5564), a ZK not-on-the-ban-list proof, a shielded pool for amounts, and a TEE or attestor-network issuer. |
| How big is this market? | Small businesses in emerging markets face a **$5.7T finance gap** (IFC). The repay-from-sales model is proven: **Shopify Capital $4.2B** a year, Moniepoint about $700M, M-Pesa's Fuliza. They do it by owning your data and your payment rails; we do it with no data, open rails, and a credit history the merchant owns. Our real bottleneck is stablecoin acceptance at the till, and the advance is the reason to switch. |
| Why World ID if the borrower is a business? | We don't use it to verify the business. The business is proven by zkTLS (a Business Pass), and one-per-human would wrongly block owners of several businesses (one human may bind several shops). World sits at the human moments. **IDKit is the first onboarding step**, so a defaulter can't come back under a new pseudonym. **Agent Pass** is used when a merchant delegates once to their managed cash-flow agent; after that, credit is fully automatic. **IDKit recovery** lets only the same human restore their private credentials on a new phone. Real World IDKit and Agent Pass are wired live on the hosted app, with test mode available as a fallback. |
| What if a merchant takes payments outside your QR? (diversion) | It can't be fully prevented. It is the main risk of any repay-from-sales product. The contract makes it costly. A merchant must repay at least 1% of owed plus fee every 7 days, whatever their QR sales. Falling behind adds a late fee (an extra 0.5% a week, capped at 3%, 100% to the reserve). After 30 days anyone can write the advance off, DRIP absorbs the loss, and the human is banned at the World ID step, so they can't return under a new shop. Exposure is small by design: first advances are $50, and limits grow only with on-time repayment. A repaid Standing Pass is worth more than one skipped advance. |
| Who are the merchants today? | Merchants in dollar-short economies, such as Argentina, Nigeria and Kenya, where people already hold stablecoins to escape inflation. The advance gives them a reason to accept stablecoins at the till. |
| Customers there use USDT, not USDC. | Live checkout takes test USDC only. Any-token checkout swaps are planned. |
| What does ZK actually hide? | Who you are (World ID is kept only as an HMAC on our server; nothing about the person goes onchain), your business details beyond yes/no predicates (the Business Pass, from zkTLS), and which repaid credential you hold (the Standing Pass). Repayment on a known shop pseudonym is still public. Hiding that needs a shielded pool, which is our roadmap. |
| Why not just use a KYC'd lender? | Contact-harvesting loan apps show what happens when lenders hold borrower data. Our privacy is built into the system, not a policy we promise to follow. Selective disclosure covers regulators. |
| Can sales be faked to get a bigger advance? | Yes, through wash trading. The demo catches some direct circular funding, but intermediary wallets and self-funded sales can evade checks. The Critic and caps do not establish genuine customers. |
| Can a prompt injection drain the pool? | No. Agent output is schema-bound, the Critic must approve independently, code clamps it, and `AdvanceBook` caps every draw onchain (tier caps, exposure cap, mandate limits, junior cover). An oversized offer reverts, and this is covered by tests. The simulator's `wash_ring` run shows a circular-funding shop rejected before any money moves. |
| What stops a defaulter from opening a new shop? | **World ID, first.** The very first onboarding step, before any business proof or shop, is World ID (IDKit, Proof of Human). Our offer signer keeps only an HMAC of the World nullifier, linked privately and encrypted to that human's shops. If any advance is written off, that human is banned: their next attempt, even with a brand-new wallet, is stopped at the World ID check (`human_banned`), before any Business Pass proof, shop or transaction (the simulator's `defaulter` run ends with exactly this). If they skipped the app and opened a shop directly, the offer signer still refuses it at offer time, before any AI is called. A human also can't hold two open advances across shops (`human_has_open_advance`). Nothing about the person goes onchain and the API never returns the nullifier. The honest limit: the ban list is enforced by our signer, not the contract; the roadmap is a ZK "not in the banned set" proof. |
| Can a defaulter come back with a new wallet? | Not as the same human: the World ID ban covers every shop they open (see above). Separately, a Standing Pass is issued only on full repayment and its nullifier prevents reuse of that credential. Someone borrowing another person's World ID remains possible, as with any proof-of-personhood system. |
| How do limits grow? | Gradually, per human. The first advance at a new limit level is capped at 50% of it (tier 1: $50 of $100); each full week of on-time minimum repayments (1% plus fee per 7 days, replayed from onchain repayment events) unlocks another 25%, so the full limit comes after two good weeks. A missed minimum freezes it; a write-off bans. |
| Is this predatory? | Merchant advances can be expensive, so the fee is small and shown upfront: 0.5% a week while you owe, never more than 3%, with slow, normal and busy repayment estimates. There are no rollovers. A bad week only slows repayment, as long as the merchant meets the 1% weekly minimum. Fall behind it and the fee doubles to 1% a week (max 6% total) until they catch up, and that extra goes 100% to the safety reserve, not to lenders. |
| Is it legal? | A real launch needs licensing in each market (for example, Kenya licenses digital lenders) and KYC through selective disclosure. AI credit decisions face extra scrutiny, including the EU AI Act's high-risk rules. |
| Isn't the anonymity set tiny? | In a demo, yes. Privacy grows with the number of repaid advances, which is why the Standing Pass should be portable across lenders. |
| What do the AI agents control? | Less than it looks. Agents hold no funds. Offers are EIP-712-signed proposals that `AdvanceBook` checks onchain against tier caps ($50/$100/$200/$300), the exposure cap, mandate limits and 20% junior cover. The Risk agent can only lower limits, never below the floors. The keeper calls `writeOff`, which anyone can call. Turn all agents off and the protocol still runs on fixed rules: payments, repayment, the late fee, write-offs and withdrawals all work. Only new advances stop. |
| Why a token? | DripCoin (DRIP) is not a platform token. It is the first-loss tranche share (ERC-4626) of the lending book. It exists for three reasons: first-loss capital that protects senior lenders, an exit for junior holders (the Uniswap v4 pool), and one early-warning signal among several (with write-offs and repayment pace). To be honest, you don't need the pool to lend. A thin AMM price is noisy and manipulable, which is why the Risk agent can only lower limits and uses three signals. |
| Is the revenue proof real? | The cryptographic path is real; the data source in the live demo is not. The live demo uses a demo seller portal and a mock attestor. The real Reclaim zkTLS path is built and tested (`docs/zktls-spike.md`, `issuer/src/reclaim.ts`). Trust model: the issuer checks the zkTLS proof, sees the seller account ID only transiently (it keeps `HMAC(pepper, account_id)` as a one-pass-per-business nullifier), and signs an attestation. The merchant then proves the pass in ZK. The issuer is a trusted party (roadmap: OPRF or TEE), and its damage is bounded by tier-0 caps, the exposure cap and junior first-loss. |
| How is this different from Stripe's "Credit on Tempo"? | Stripe's Credit on Tempo (launched 5 Nov 2026) lets businesses borrow from third-party lenders on stablecoin rails; the lenders underwrite, and "Tempo is not a lender". DripMoney differs in four ways. It is private: ZK credentials plus World ID, with no identity onchain or with us. It is built for tiny informal shops ($50 to $300 advances). Repayment lives inside the payment (8% of each QR sale). And a merchant's agent draws within limits they delegate once. |
